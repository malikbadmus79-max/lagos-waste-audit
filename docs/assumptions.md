# Assumptions

Log of assumptions made in the analysis, referenced by ID wherever they are used in code, notebooks or reports.

| ID | Assumption | Reason | Used in | Sensitivity |
|----|------------|--------|---------|-------------|
| A01 | Monthly and weekly totals are converted to daily averages by dividing by the number of calendar days in the period, counting both end dates (31 days for May 2026; 7 days for 28 July to 3 August 2026). | Official statements do not say whether weekends and public holidays are operating days. | `lagos_waste.official_stats.daily_average`; notebook 01 | Using 26 operating days for May 2026 would raise the daily average from 13,500 to 16,096 t/day, widening the gap with stated PSP capacity. |
| A02 | Truck trips to disposal facilities carry 10 tonnes each on average. | In S02, both tonnage figures equal exactly 10 tonnes per trip (18,660 / 1,866 and 3,490 / 349), which indicates that official tonnage is derived from trip counts at this rate. | Conversion of the 2021 trip counts in S07; notebook 01 | At 8 or 12 tonnes per trip, 850 daily trips in 2021 would equal 6,800 or 10,200 t/day instead of 8,500 t/day. |
| A03 | Point-in-time statements (operator counts, fleet sizes, generation estimates) refer to the publication date of the source. | Sources rarely state the reference date of a count. | `data/processed/official_stats.csv` (period_start and period_end) | Shifts dates by weeks, not years; no effect on the comparisons in notebook 01. |
| A04 | Daily waste generation is 13,000 to 15,000 t/day throughout 2021 to 2026. | This is the most frequently stated official range [S03, S06, S09]; no source reports a measured series. | Ratios of evacuation to generation in notebook 01 | At the 16,000 t/day upper value in S08, the May 2026 evacuation figure would equal 84% of generation instead of 90% to 104%. |
| A05 | Budget values are approved appropriations in nominal naira, not actual spending and not adjusted for inflation. | Budget implementation reports have not yet been reviewed, and no deflator has been sourced. | Budget chart in notebook 01 | Real growth is lower than nominal growth over 2021 to 2026, given high consumer price inflation over the period. |
| A06 | Days since last collection are estimated from the Q10 time bands at band midpoints: 4 days (within 7 days), 11 days (8 to 14), 22.5 days (15 to 30) and 45 days (more than a month). "Never collected" and "Can't remember" receive no value. | Q10 records time bands, not dates; the open upper band has no natural midpoint, and 45 days is used as a conservative value. | `lagos_waste.survey` (`days_since_collection`); notebook 02 | Average gaps depend on the 45-day value; the share of households with a gap over 14 days (`gap_over_14_days`) does not use midpoints and is the main measure. |
| A07 | Repeated reports of one event are approximated by counting distinct LGA and ISO-week pairs: several service-failure articles about the same LGA in the same calendar week count once. | Outlets often publish more than one article from a single briefing or incident; articles do not carry an event identifier. | `lagos_waste.news_analysis.lga_weeks`; notebook 02 | Two distinct events in one LGA in one week are counted once, and one event reported across two weeks is counted twice. |
| A08 | Households on a weekly truck schedule expect 4 collections in 4 weeks, fortnightly 2 and monthly 1. Missed collections equal expected minus reported collections, with a floor of 0. | Q8 records the stated schedule and Q9 the collections in the last 4 weeks. | `lagos_waste.survey` (`expected_pickups_4wk`, `missed_pickups_4wk`); notebook 02 | A 4-week window can contain a fifth weekly collection day; reports of 5 or more collections therefore show no missed collection. |
| A10 | Areas named in the PSP directory are assigned to constitutional LGAs by the standard LCDA-to-LGA correspondence (for example Igando-Ikotun, Egbe Idimu and Agbado Oke Odo to Alimosho; Ikoyi-Obalende and Iru/VI to Eti-Osa; Ojokoro to Ifako-Ijaiye; Ojodu and Onigbongbo to Ikeja). An operator listed in several areas of one LGA is counted once for that LGA. | The directory is organised by LGA and LCDA; the analysis unit is the LGA. | `lagos_waste.coverage.AREA_TO_LGA`; notebook 03 | Operators serving areas in two LGAs are counted in both, so LGA counts sum to 451 against 431 distinct operators. |
| A09 | Distance to disposal is measured as the straight-line distance from each LGA's representative point to the nearest of the three active sites, in kilometres (UTM zone 31N). | Road network routing for Lagos was not run; truck routes and queue times are not available. | `lagos_waste.sites.distance_to_nearest`; notebook 03 | Straight lines cross the lagoon, so distances for Eti-Osa, Lagos Island, Apapa and Ibeju-Lekki understate road distance; LGA-level points also ignore distances within large LGAs such as Ikorodu and Epe. |
| A11 | Solous and Ewu-Elepe are located at the unnamed OpenStreetMap landfill polygons nearest to Igando (way 705802151, about 3.0 km from the Igando place point) and to the Elepe community (way 785706979, about 2.1 km away). | Neither site is named in OpenStreetMap; both are reported in Igando and Elepe [S22, S24], and no other landfill polygon lies near either place. | `lagos_waste.sites.ACTIVE_SITES`; notebook 03 | A location error of a few kilometres changes LGA distances only slightly against a range of 1.5 to 52.4 km. |
